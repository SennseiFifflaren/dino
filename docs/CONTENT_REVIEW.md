# Phase 1 content review

Ten original beginner questions cover easily recognized body features, diets, and movement. Each includes a Natural History Museum, London Dino Directory attribution and a species reference URL. No source prose or external artwork is bundled. Basic facts were reviewed for ambiguity; uncertain behaviour and feature functions are phrased cautiously.

| ID suffix | Intended fact | Why the alternatives are unsuitable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| three-horns | Triceratops: three horns and a bony frill | The other listed genera lack this combination. |
| rex-diet | Tyrannosaurus rex was carnivorous | Leaves, grass and fruit are not its established diet. |
| back-plates | Stegosaurus had large dorsal plates | Other listed genera did not have those plates. No claim of a settled plate function. |
| long-neck | Brachiosaurus was a long-necked sauropod | The other listed genera did not have a very long sauropod neck. |
| tail-club | Ankylosaurus had a bony tail club | The other listed genera did not have that structure. Defence is described as a possible use. |
| plant-eater | Diplodocus was herbivorous | Fish, insects and other dinosaurs are unsuitable categories for its established diet. |
| sail-back | Spinosaurus had tall dorsal spines | Other listed genera lacked that sail-like structure. Its function is left open. |
| thumb-spike | Iguanodon had thumb spikes | Other listed genera lacked these large thumb spikes. |
| rex-legs | Tyrannosaurus rex was bipedal | Four, six and eight are incorrect counts for walking legs. |
| triceratops-diet | Triceratops was herbivorous | Horns do not imply carnivory; leaves suit its plant diet. |

Reference pages: `https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dino-directory/` followed by `triceratops.html`, `tyrannosaurus.html`, `stegosaurus.html`, `brachiosaurus.html`, `ankylosaurus.html`, `diplodocus.html`, `spinosaurus.html`, and `iguanodon.html`.
