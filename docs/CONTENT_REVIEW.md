# Scientific content review — five levels

Fifty original questions are curated in increasing difficulty, with exactly ten per level. Level 1 stable IDs and wording are retained for save compatibility. The bank avoids absolute size rankings and disputed behaviour claims, distinguishes pterosaurs/marine reptiles from dinosaurs, and explicitly recognizes birds as living theropod dinosaurs. Explanations are original writing, not copied museum descriptions.

## Scientific review

- Beginner: recognizable anatomy, herbivore/carnivore diets and movement.
- Explorer: food, armour, tail defence, front/back leg proportions, eggs, upright posture and animal groups.
- Researcher: period order, non-overlapping lifetimes, fossil locations, Pangaea and the approximately 66-million-year end-Cretaceous extinction.
- Scientist: body/trace fossils, sedimentary preservation, minerals, birds/theropods, field context, preservation bias and coprolites.
- Expert: limits of footprint/colour/feather evidence, Dimetrodon and marine reptile classification, index fossils, incomplete evidence, ancestry and revising explanations.

Careful wording: feathers do not imply flight; birds evolved within theropods rather than directly from T. rex; bones alone usually cannot establish colour; a single track may not identify a species; Spinosaurus sail function is left uncertain. Argentinosaurus is very large without an absolute size ranking. Wrong options and hints were reviewed to avoid multiple scientifically defensible choices or naming the correct option.
